# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

desktop (Unity 6 URP, macOS and Windows builds; mouse and keyboard)

## Users

- International students in their first weeks in Seoul who must get through counter visits in Korean before they can speak it. Primary persona: a 20-year-old Vietnamese D-4 language-course student, in their first six months.
- At the ITB x CAU hackathon (3 days, no theme, prototype and pitch): judges who see a short demo and the pitch. Earlier, at the Global Startup Competition 2026 (Vietnam, 8–11 November 2026): judges and visitors who played a short session on a laptop at the booth while others watched over their shoulder.

## Product Purpose

Ready Set Go! (renamed on 2026-11-25 for the ITB x CAU hackathon; earlier Ready Set Seoul, Be Korean! and the working title Seoul First Week) lets a student rehearse the flow of real first-week situations — arriving at the airport, the subway, a clinic, the immigration office, a bank, an emergency — by walking a Myeongdong street in first person, taking a queue number and choosing what to say to Korean-speaking staff. Success: the player walks into the real office knowing the order of things, the documents to bring and the phrases that work.

## Positioning

"We train the situation, not the vocabulary." Translators handle words and language apps teach vocabulary; this rehearses a whole counter visit, with its pressure, its order and its realistic mistakes.

## Operating Context

- Sessions of 5–15 minutes per part; a booth demo plays one part start to finish in front of an audience, often in a noisy hall (subtitles and translations are always on screen).
- Every staff line and answer is shown in Korean with romanization and a translation in the player's language (Vietnamese, Indonesian or English).
- Finished parts go into a Logbook the player can reread instead of replaying.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Conversations are multiple choice (2–4 answers, keys 1–4), and the player may say an answer out loud instead (hold Space; offline Whisper picks the answer said and shows how well it came across). No typing and no points score.
- A visit can be personalised: the player picks their situation (clinic: a fever and a sore throat, or a stomach ache) and the visit's questions and answers follow it.
- Wrong answers are realistic learner mistakes with a short lesson; asking for help is never a mistake.
- Levels, dialogue, documents and dependencies are data (procedure JSON); scenes are generated by code.
- Voices are pre-rendered offline; the game must run without a network.
- Institutions and brands are fictional; procedure content stays marked unverified until a Korean teammate checks it.

## Brand Commitments

- Name: **Ready Set Go!** (chosen by the user on 2026-11-25 for the ITB x CAU hackathon; "highfive" is the team's name, not the product's). Before that: **Ready Set Seoul**, Korean subtitle 서울 첫 주, set by the user on 2026-10-19. It replaced "Be Korean!", which was too close to the "Be Koready" label of Koready, a Korean company in the same niche. Its scope is deliberately specific: an international student's first week in Seoul.
- Art direction pinned by the user to `references/art/`: comic and graphic-novel ink (brush-stroke banners, bold poster type, halftone, inked icons), a retro, slightly cartoon look with ink line edges in 3D as well. Revised by the user on 2026-10-18: firmer and more highlighted, less papery, a bit retro ("Retro Ink").
- One colour palette, Signboard, sampled from the 3D street: shop-fascia teal for the active thing, late-sun gold for highlights, blue-violet ink and night, warm paper. The user chose on 2026-11-25 to pull the UI towards the 3D world because the earlier warm Persimmon palette made the UI look like a different game (the second theme was dropped on 2026-10-18).
- Small text, above all non-Korean text, must be solid and easy to read.
- The environment should feel a little fantasy: dramatic warm light and a sky with clouds.
- Motion must feel hand-made and specific to this world, not stock fades and slides.
- The whole game (UI and 3D) must read as one polished, coherent game.

## Candidate Ideas (ITB x CAU hackathon, not decided)

Ideas the team raised on 2026-11-25. The user then decided (same day) on this scope for the build: rename to Ready Set Go!, say the phrase out loud (idea 1), the new places (ideas 5 and 6, built on 2026-11-25 from `references/RESEARCH-NEW-SCENARIOS.md`), personalised visits (idea 3), and the UI pulled towards the 3D world. Until idea 1 is built, the working rules in `CLAUDE.md` still hold (multiple choice only, no push-to-talk, no score, no real brands).

1. **Say it out loud.** After choosing an answer (or instead of clicking it), the player speaks the Korean line and gets an accuracy score with the parts they missed. For the demo, use offline Whisper (whisper.unity, or Whisper-Tiny on Unity's Inference Engine, which is already in the project). It transcribes the speech to Hangul and compares it with the target line jamo by jamo. That measures whether the line is understood, not true pronunciation quality. Phoneme-level scoring (e.g. Azure Pronunciation Assessment, online and paid) goes in the pitch as the roadmap. A low score could make the staff ask "네? 다시 한번만…". Clicking must stay as a fallback for noisy halls. Needs the team to lift the "no push-to-talk / no score" rule.
2. **Brand partnerships (revenue model, not for the prototype).** Real stores and brands in the game for a fee. Signage is already generated from data (`FacadeDresser`), so brand slots are easy to add. A stronger version is sponsored scenarios: a real bank in "Open a bank account", a telecom in "Get a SIM card", since those companies already target international students. Risks: the procedures must stay neutral and accurate, with the sponsor clearly labelled. The current "fictional brands only" rule is there for the demo; a commercial version replaces it with licensed trademarks.
3. **Personalised clinic or hospital visit.** Before the visit the player sets their situation: what is wrong (e.g. a cold, a stomach ache, a sprained ankle), their insurance status, and special needs (an allergy, needing a medical certificate). These choices change the dialogue, the treatment path and the bill, and the results screen explains what they paid and why. This fits the existing data model (flags with `needs` / `unless` / `grants` in procedure JSON). The main risk is the number of combinations to write, verify and voice, so keep the branch points few. Medical and cost facts must be verified like every procedure, and the game rehearses the visit; it never gives medical advice.
4. **Know before you go.** At the start of every scenario, a short briefing card with the general facts a student should know about that place: opening hours and lunch break, whether to book ahead, what to bring, a rough cost, one local tip. Keep it to about three facts so it doesn't break "one thing at a time", and let at least one of them matter later in the visit. It can share data with the Logbook's "Before you go" tab, so the facts are written once in the procedure or level data. Hours and costs vary by place and must be verified and phrased as "usually".
5. **New scenarios: airport and subway.** Built 2026-11-25: the airport arrival (immigration, baggage, customs; the airport bus ticket) and 명동역 (buy and charge a transport card, the gate, the direction, the train; a bag left on the train).
6. **Emergency scenario.** Built 2026-11-25 as a 3D situation: a 119 call for a man who fell on 명동길 (the dispatcher on the phone, the ambulance) and a night ER (desk, form, triage, payment). Staff only ask and register; no first aid, diagnosis or medicine advice. A night pharmacy was left out because its visit centres on medicine advice; a police box (lost wallet) remains an alternative.

Dropped by the user on 2026-11-25, don't propose again: real-world missions, subtitles that fade as the player improves, non-verbal etiquette as a feature, staff with different personalities.

## Evidence on Hand

- Real game content: procedure files in `Assets/_Project/Data/Procedures/`, levels in `Resources/levels.json`, baked Korean voices, an OSM-derived Myeongdong street.
- Local play telemetry (`levels.csv`) from the team's own play-tests only. No user-study results, testimonials or learning-outcome data exist yet; do not invent any.

## Product Principles

1. The situation is the lesson: show the order of a real visit, not word lists.
2. Mistakes teach, never punish: a wrong answer gets a real reaction and a short note, and the player tries again.
3. Korean first: Korean is the largest text in every exchange, with help directly beneath it.
4. One thing at a time: the screen asks for one decision or one action.
5. Detail where the player looks: the counters, papers and people within reach carry the craft.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- Readable at a glance from a booth distance; Korean, romanization and translation always shown together.
- State is carried by shape and marks as well as colour (a circle for right, a slash for wrong), so nothing depends on colour vision.
- Reading text is solid (never translucent) and bold at small sizes; text over the 3D scene sits on a dark plate.
- A reduced-motion setting must calm the hand-drawn animation (assumption: not yet confirmed with the user).
