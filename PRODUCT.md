# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

desktop (Unity 6 URP, macOS and Windows builds; mouse and keyboard)

## Users

- International students in their first weeks in Seoul who must get through counter visits in Korean before they can speak it. Primary persona: a 20-year-old Vietnamese D-4 language-course student, in their first six months.
- At the Global Startup Competition 2026 (Vietnam, 8–11 November 2026): judges and visitors who play a short session on a laptop at the booth while others watch over their shoulder.

## Product Purpose

Ready Set Seoul (named on 2026-10-19; earlier Be Korean! and the working title Seoul First Week) lets a student rehearse the flow of real first-week visits — a clinic, the immigration office, a bank — by walking a Myeongdong street in first person, taking a queue number and choosing what to say to Korean-speaking staff. Success: the player walks into the real office knowing the order of things, the documents to bring and the phrases that work.

## Positioning

"We train the situation, not the vocabulary." Translators handle words and language apps teach vocabulary; this rehearses a whole counter visit, with its pressure, its order and its realistic mistakes.

## Operating Context

- Sessions of 5–15 minutes per part; a booth demo plays one part start to finish in front of an audience, often in a noisy hall (subtitles and translations are always on screen).
- Every staff line and answer is shown in Korean with romanization and a translation in the player's language (Vietnamese, Indonesian or English).
- Finished parts go into a Logbook the player can reread instead of replaying.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Conversations are multiple choice (2–4 answers, keys 1–4); no typing, voice input or points score.
- Wrong answers are realistic learner mistakes with a short lesson; asking for help is never a mistake.
- Levels, dialogue, documents and dependencies are data (procedure JSON); scenes are generated by code.
- Voices are pre-rendered offline; the game must run without a network.
- Institutions and brands are fictional; procedure content stays marked unverified until a Korean teammate checks it.

## Brand Commitments

- Name: **Ready Set Seoul**, Korean subtitle 서울 첫 주. Set by the user on 2026-10-19. It replaced "Be Korean!", which was too close to the "Be Koready" label of Koready, a Korean company in the same niche. Its scope is deliberately specific: an international student's first week in Seoul.
- Art direction pinned by the user to `references/art/`: comic and graphic-novel ink (brush-stroke banners, bold poster type, halftone, inked icons), a retro, slightly cartoon look with ink line edges in 3D as well. Revised by the user on 2026-10-18: firmer and more highlighted, less papery, a bit retro ("Retro Ink").
- One colour palette, Persimmon, built from closely related (analogous) warm colours with near-black ink and cream (the user dropped the second theme on 2026-10-18).
- Small text, above all non-Korean text, must be solid and easy to read.
- The environment should feel a little fantasy: dramatic warm light and a sky with clouds.
- Motion must feel hand-made and specific to this world, not stock fades and slides.
- The whole game (UI and 3D) must read as one polished, coherent game.

## Evidence on Hand

- Real game content: procedure files in `Assets/_Project/Data/Procedures/`, levels in `Resources/levels.json`, baked Korean voices, an OSM-derived Myeongdong street.
- Local play telemetry (`levels.csv`) from the team's own play-tests only. No user-study results, testimonials or learning-outcome data exist yet; do not invent any.

## Product Principles

1. The situation is the lesson: show the order of a real visit, not word lists.
2. Mistakes teach, never punish: a wrong answer gets a real reaction and a short note, and the player tries again.
3. Korean first: Korean is the largest text in every exchange, with help directly beneath it.
4. One thing at a time: the screen asks for one decision or one action.
5. Detail where the player looks: the counters, papers and people within reach carry the craft.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- Readable at a glance from a booth distance; Korean, romanization and translation always shown together.
- State is carried by shape and marks as well as colour (a circle for right, a slash for wrong), so nothing depends on colour vision.
- Reading text is solid (never translucent) and bold at small sizes; text over the 3D scene sits on a dark plate.
- A reduced-motion setting must calm the hand-drawn animation (assumption: not yet confirmed with the user).
